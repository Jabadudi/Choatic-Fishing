# Boat Captain - Character Sheet

**Role:** Old boatman who ferries Eli to the "island."

**First appearance:** Chapter 1 (referenced in Chapter 2)

## Appearance (as stated)
- An old man; first seen mending a net on an upturned crate at the port (Ch1).
- "weathered face"; his grin "cracked his whole weathered face open" (Ch1).
- His boat: a battered dinghy that "looked like it had survived several wars and lost most of them" (Ch1).

## Personality and voice
Friendly and teasing. He laughs at Eli's seasickness. Sincerely grieves Oscar. His sorrow fades slowly, "like the tide going out." His professionalism is questionable: he wrote his number on Eli's hand in marker and left without setting a return time.
- "My, how you've grown. Last time I saw you, you were about knee-high and sick over the side of my boat the whole way out." (Ch1)
- "Ah, hell. I'm sorry, son. He was a good one. Best fisherman I ever knew, and I've known plenty." (Ch1)
- "You'll get your sea legs." (Ch1)

## Relationships
- **Grandpa Oscar:** Old friend. Oscar told him Eli might come someday and that he was not to overcharge "the kid."
- **Eli:** Recognized him on sight. He had taken Eli out as a child.

## What he knows / hooks
- Recognized Eli without being told his name.
- Charged about a third of the usual price. Eli calls this "my first warning."
- The two-hour trip ends at a place Mara says "is no island." Whether the captain knows where he really took Eli is unknown.
- His phone number is on the back of Eli's hand.

## Open questions / not yet established
- Name, exact age, eye/hair details, clothing.
- Whether he knows about the other world or Oscar's secret.
- Whether he will return, or can.

## Visual reference notes
Old, weathered harbor fisherman with a big grin, mending nets on a crate. Pair him with a ramshackle little dinghy. Clothing and coloring are undefined.
