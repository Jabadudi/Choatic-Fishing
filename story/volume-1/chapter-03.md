# Chapter 3 — The Village

It was a short walk.

Short for her, anyway. She moved through the trees like she had a map of every root in her head, and I followed her like a man who had been electrocuted, knocked unconscious, and left to sleep in a wet field. Which I had.

The path climbed away from the shore and wound north through the forest. Somewhere along the way we passed under a crooked branch hung with three old wooden chimes. A breeze came off the water and knocked them together, one low, hollow note after another, and the sound followed us a lot further into the trees than sound is supposed to.

I didn't ask.

I was building quite a collection of things I didn't ask about.

Then the trees thinned out, and there was the village.

⸻

It wasn't exactly a city.

It was more or less what you'd picture if someone said *village* and gave you about three seconds to imagine it. A gravel-and-dirt road running up the middle. Houses of timber and stone with steep roofs and smoke curling out of their chimneys. A square with a well. Fields off to the east, and docks running down to the water on the south side, boats bumping gently against the posts.

And it was alive.

Not just busy. *Alive.* People hauling nets and carrying baskets and shouting across lanes at each other. Someone hammering something. Someone else yelling at whoever was hammering. Kids chasing each other around the well in the specific screaming way kids do when nobody has told them about the monster attack last night, or when somebody has and they've decided it's the most exciting thing that's ever happened.

There were all kinds of people, too. Pale and freckled, brown and weathered, tall, short, broad, wiry, every shade and build you could think of, all crammed together like the world had tipped its whole box of people out onto one little patch of shore. A couple of the girls by the well were extremely my type, which was a thought I decided, very firmly, to keep to myself.

I had not survived the night just to get stabbed by my rescuer for staring.

Then I noticed the animals.

There were a lot of animals.

None of them were normal.

Lying across the doorway of what I was pretty sure was a blacksmith's shop was something I wanted to call a dog. It was roughly the size of a Tibetan mastiff, with the same heavy mane of fur around its neck and shoulders, except that from the shoulders back the fur gave way to *scales.* Thick ones. Blue-gray and overlapping, like armor plates. Frost had gathered in the grooves between them, even though the morning wasn't cold.

It opened one eye as we passed and looked at me.

I kept walking.

On the roof above it, something the size of a bobcat was cleaning its paw. It had the tufted ears. It had the stubby tail. It also had a pair of leathery, bat-like wings folded neatly along its back, and when a bird landed too close to it on the roof, it unfolded them with a snap and launched itself, and the bird very quickly had a much worse morning than mine.

They were some ugly animals.

Genuinely. They looked like someone had been told to draw a dog and a cat from memory, and their memory was bad, and they'd been drinking.

And I would've given anything for them to be a dream.

*Somebody here is running an illegal crossbreeding operation,* I thought. *That's the only explanation. Somebody's in a barn with a lizard and a dog and absolutely no adult supervision.*

I pinched myself on the back of the hand. Hard.

"Ow."

"What's wrong?" she asked, glancing back.

"Nothing." I rubbed my hand. Real. Annoyingly real. "What's with the… uh. Those." I pointed at the mastiff-thing in the doorway, as discreetly as you can point at something that big.

"The big one is a Rimejaw," she said. "The little one with wings is a Flitlynx." She gave me a long, sideways look. "I would stay away from the Rimejaw if I were you."

"Wasn't planning on hugging it."

"Good. They're extremely territorial, and their bite freezes whatever it catches. It took us generations to tame them." She nodded at the one in the doorway like you'd nod at a colleague. "They fought with us last night, alongside the Moonfang. They're very useful in a fight. But even a beast like that can't hold off the shadow walkers alone. Not for long."

There it was again.

*Shadow walkers.*

"Okay," I said. "What *are* they? The shadow walkers. Because I saw one, and I'd like to know what could take on a… a grim-jaw."

"Rimejaw," she corrected.

"That's what I said."

"It isn't."

She didn't slow down, but her voice changed when she started explaining. Got flatter. Quieter. The way people talk when they're repeating something they were taught as kids and never once found funny.

"The shadow walkers are ancient. They walked this land long before we did, and they come in every shape and size. They're what's left of the dark army that served Croshelda, the Witch of Darkness." She stepped around a puddle without looking at it. "Four hundred years ago, Goldanir disappeared from this region. That's when the shadow walkers began coming back. They want to devour everything in their path, and they don't stop."

"And they're strong."

"They take the power of whatever they infect," she said, "and multiply it. Several times over." She was quiet for a step or two. "Last night, we were lucky. We only lost one Rimejaw. No one else."

She said *lucky* like she meant it.

I looked back at the frost-scaled monster in the doorway. Then I thought about one of those things getting *infected*, and becoming several times worse, and I decided to stop thinking about it.

It didn't work.

A witch of darkness. Animals I had never seen before in my life. Shadow walkers that stole powers. And some guy named Goldanir who had apparently spent centuries keeping the monsters away, and then just… left.

What was this? Some kind of fantasy world?

*Where the hell am I?*
