# Chapter 5 — Sunrise

The guest lodge was a long, low building behind the chief's cabin, and my room was small and plain. A bed with a straw mattress and a heavy wool blanket. A window with real glass, slightly warped. A stool.

Mara gave me the tour, which took about four seconds.

"Bed," she said, pointing. "Window. Chamber pot."

I looked at the chamber pot.

The chamber pot looked back.

I decided that, like a lot of things in my life right now, that was a problem for future me.

She set a leather waterskin down on the stool.

"This is all you need for tonight," she said, heading for the door. "I'll see you in the morning. *Sunrise,* Eli."

"Sunrise," I agreed.

She closed the door behind her, and I was alone.

⸻

It was already late. The window had gone from gold to violet to black while I sat on the edge of the bed, not doing anything, my hand resting on my pocket over the lure.

Still warm.

Still pulsing, slow and steady, a little faster whenever I thought too hard about anything.

Tomorrow was a new day. Tomorrow I'd start training for something I didn't understand, in a world I didn't understand, so I could find a mountain I'd never seen and some stones I couldn't describe, and get home.

I had to get home.

Shylee was there. My little sister. Sitting on the couch, probably, checking her phone for a text I'd promised to send the second I got to the island.

I never sent it.

She'd be wondering why. Then she'd be worried. And then, sooner or later, she'd be the only one left in that apartment, waiting for someone who might never come back, the same way Grandpa used to sit on that dock and wait.

*Love you, dummy.*

I had to get back to her.

But lying there in the dark, staring at a ceiling in another world, I couldn't shake it.

The excitement.

Grandpa had been here. He'd walked this village, sat at that table, drunk that tea. He'd been strong, and extraordinary, and he'd come home with stories that were true. And now I was here too.

I couldn't sleep.

So I did what every normal human being does when they can't sleep.

I closed my eyes and pictured sheep jumping over a fence, and started counting.

One sheep.

Two sheep.

Three sheep.

Four shee…

Fiiive shhh…

*Snnnrrrk.*

⸻

I woke up drenched in sweat.

The room was still dark. My heart was pounding like I'd been running, and something heavy was sitting on my chest. Not a thing. A feeling. Dread, thick and shapeless, the kind that follows you out of a nightmare you can't remember.

Then I heard it.

A voice.

The same voice from the cabin last night. Soft and clear and impossibly gentle, like an angel had leaned down right next to my ear.

"Accept me."

I lay perfectly still.

"You have to save her."

Slowly, I turned my head.

My jeans were draped over the stool where I'd left them, and through the fabric of the pocket, the lure was glowing. Blue. Pulsing.

Bright.

Dim.

Bright.

Same rhythm as my heartbeat. Exactly the same.

I don't remember deciding to get up. I just did. I crossed the room, pulled it out of my pocket, and held it in my hand.

"Ugh. My head."

The pain came out of nowhere, a spike driven straight in behind my eyes. The room tilted. The floor went soft. And then I wasn't in the room anymore.

I was somewhere else.

Almost a dream. Not quite.

There was a girl.

She had long white hair, pearly and shining, falling all the way down her back, and her eyes were purple. A deep, unreal purple, like the sky right before a storm.

She was crying.

I didn't know her. I'd never seen her before in my life. But the second I saw her crying, something in my chest pulled so hard it hurt, and I reached for her without thinking.

Black light exploded out of her.

Not light. The opposite of light. A darkness that *moved*, an aura that burst outward from her body like a shockwave and swallowed everything around us.

Then something grabbed me.

Something huge. I never saw it. I just felt it close around me and rip me off the ground, and suddenly I was being hauled up into the sky, higher and higher, the girl shrinking away beneath me into all that blackness.

I screamed for her.

I didn't know her. I didn't know her name.

But I screamed it anyway.

"*Crosheldaaaa!*"

⸻

"*Aaaah!*"

I jolted awake.

I was on the floor of my room, on my back, staring up at the ceiling, sweating and shaking and gulping air like I'd been drowning.

*What was that?*

It hadn't felt like a dream. Dreams go blurry the second you wake up. This hadn't. I could still see her hair. Her eyes. The tears on her face.

It felt like a memory.

Or a premonition.

Or something worse that I didn't have a word for yet.

*Croshelda.*

The Witch of Darkness.

"Save her," the voice whispered.

I looked down at my hand. I was still holding the lure. It was glowing, steady and patient, a limp-noodle-looking glowing thing that apparently held the power of an entire region and wanted to live in my stomach.

"Ingest it, huh…?"

I sat up.

I looked at it for a long time.

Then I raised it over my mouth, said my prayers, and dropped it in.

One slurp.

One gulp.

I downed that thing like a shot of tequila.

For about one second, nothing happened.

Then everything did.

"*Ugh!*" My head felt like it was exploding. My stomach clenched like I'd swallowed a live coal. "My head… my *stomach*… *Ugh!*"

Lights bloomed in front of my eyes. Little glowing shapes, hovering in the air in front of my face. Writing. Actual *writing*, lines of it, floating there in the dark, and I couldn't read a single word of it because the room wouldn't stop spinning.

Something moved in the corner.

An ocelot. Spotted. Sleek. Sitting there like it owned the place.

It opened its mouth.

And it *talked.*

I tried my best not to pass out.

I really did.

I passed out.
