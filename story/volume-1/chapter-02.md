# Chapter 2 — Shadow Walker

"Are you finally awake?"

I was not.

Not really. My body was technically conscious, but my brain was still somewhere back in the mud, buffering.

I stared at her. She stared back, one eyebrow slightly raised, turning Grandpa's rod over the flames so the fish on the end of it browned evenly on both sides.

It was a weirdly competent thing to be doing at a moment like this.

Then it all came back at once.

The storm.

The lightning tearing the roof open.

The man with the axe, walking toward the cabin one slow step at a time, his face nothing but shadow.

The lure, burning in my pocket like a coal, and the shock that ripped through every muscle in my body and dropped me face-first into the mud.

And the wolf. Gray. Huge. Coming out of the dark like a thrown boulder.

I sat up too fast. My head swam. Every part of me ached in a slightly different way, like my body was trying to file a separate complaint for each muscle.

"Easy," she said.

I rubbed my face with both hands, as if I could scrub the night off it. It didn't work. When I lowered them, she was still there, and the fire was still there, and I was still sitting on a blanket in the wet grass with no idea how I had gotten there.

It was morning. Barely. The sky was the pale, washed-out gray that comes after a storm has spent everything it has. Mist hung low between the trees, and somewhere nearby, water was lapping softly against wood. The air smelled like rain and woodsmoke and fish, and, underneath all of it, faintly, like something that had been burned.

Behind her, through the mist, I could make out the shape of the cabin.

Or what was left of it.

A whole corner of the roof was gone. Splintered beams stuck up into the air like broken ribs, still black where the lightning had hit them. One wall leaned at an angle walls are not supposed to lean.

Grandpa's cabin.

I'd been there one day.

"Good thing I got to you in time last night," she said, turning the fish again. "You could have been dead if that shadow walker had reached you."

I looked at her.

She said it the way you'd say *good thing you brought an umbrella*. Casually. Like *shadow walker* was a normal pair of words that normal people used in sentences.

"Shadow walker?" I said.

My voice came out rough and cracked. I cleared my throat and tried again, but I had nothing better to follow it up with, so I just said it again.

"Shadow walker?"

"The thing with the axe." She frowned, like I was being slow on purpose. "My home village was attacked last night. A whole pack of them came out of the forest after dark. We fought them off together with the Moonfang."

I opened my mouth.

I closed it again.

"…The what?"

"The Moonfang," she repeated, perfectly serious. "The wolves. They roam the land and keep its peace from the monsters of the night." She gestured vaguely at the trees with the fishing rod, as though they might be standing right there. "They've guarded the villages for as long as anyone remembers."

I had several questions.

Most of them involved the word *wolves*.

I decided, for the sake of my sanity, to save those for later.

"Anyway," she went on, "near the end of the fight, I noticed one of them breaking away from the others. It wasn't running from us. It was heading for the shore, like it was going somewhere specific. So I chased it down." She shrugged. "And before I knew it, you were its target."

*Its target.*

I thought about the shape in the lightning. The axe. The way it hadn't hurried. It hadn't needed to.

The way it had come straight for *me.*

A cold, crawling feeling worked its way down the back of my neck, and it had nothing to do with the wet grass.

Something warm was still pressed against my thigh. I slid my hand into my pocket, slowly, and closed my fingers around the lure.

Still there.

Still warm.

I didn't take it out.

I don't know why, exactly. I just had the strong, simple feeling that I shouldn't. Not yet. Not in front of her.

I looked at her properly for the first time.

She was about my age, maybe a little younger. Long dark hair, still damp, pulled back and tied with what looked like a strip of leather. Mud on her boots. Mud on her sleeves. A thin scratch across one cheekbone that she didn't seem to have noticed. She looked like someone who had spent the whole night fighting monsters and then gone fishing afterward, which, apparently, was exactly what she'd done.

She'd been worried. That much was obvious, under everything else. She was also clearly annoyed with me for reasons I couldn't work out, and, I couldn't help noticing, kind of cute about it.

Then something changed in her face.

I watched it happen.

The worry went first. Then the annoyance. For about a second and a half, there was nothing there at all, just a perfectly blank stillness, like someone who had just heard a sound in an empty house.

Then her eyes went very wide.

Then they narrowed.

She looked at me. She looked over her shoulder, at the broken cabin. She looked back at me.

"Wait."

The fish was starting to burn. She didn't notice.

"What were you even doing in my grandma's old home anyway?"

I blinked.

"Your *grandma's* home?" It came out more defensive than I meant it to, but I'd had a very long night. "This is my *grandpa's* fishing cabin!"

She stared at me.

I stared at her.

"He left it to me," I said. "I've got a map. With his handwriting on it. I came out here yesterday, on a boat, which, by the way, was the worst two hours of my entire—"

I stopped.

*Boat.*

The word landed in my brain like a stone dropping into a well, and I realized a few things all at once.

The captain wasn't coming back. Not anytime soon, anyway. I had never actually arranged for him to come back.

My phone didn't work.

The cabin had been struck by lightning.

Something with an axe and no face had walked out of a storm specifically to kill me, and the only reason I was still alive was a wolf the size of a pony and a girl I'd never met, who was now looking at me like *I* was the weird one.

And I was on an island.

With no way off it.

"Speaking of boats," I said. "I need one."

"A boat?"

"A boat. Any boat. I'm not picky." I was already trying to get up. My legs had opinions about that, and I ignored them. "Canoe. Raft. A really big log. I need to get off this island as soon as possible."

She didn't move.

She just looked at me for a long moment, with an expression I couldn't read at all.

"Island?" she said slowly.

"Yes. *Island.*" I waved an arm at, well, everything. "Island. Water all the way around it. Very small. Lots of trees. One cabin, recently exploded."

"This is no island," she said.

The words just sort of sat there between us.

Somewhere in the mist, out on the water I couldn't see, a bird called. Long and low and lonely.

I laughed. It came out wrong.

"Okay. Funny. I took a boat here. From the coast. For two hours. I *felt* every one of those two hours."

She didn't laugh.

She set Grandpa's rod down carefully across two stones by the fire, fish and all, and stood up. She brushed the mud off her knees, and she looked out toward the water, and then she looked back at me, and something in her face had gone quiet and careful. The way people look at you right before they tell you something you're not going to like.

"If you really are his grandson," she said, "then I don't know how to help you get back."

My stomach dropped all the way to my shoes.

"Back," I repeated. "Back where?"

She didn't answer that.

Instead, she held out her hand to help me up.

"But I know someone who might," she said. "Come with me."
