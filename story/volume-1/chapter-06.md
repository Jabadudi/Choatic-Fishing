# Chapter 6 — Resonance

Somebody was trying to break down my door.

*Bang. Bang. Bang.*

"Hey!! Eli! Wake up!!"

I opened my eyes.

Sunlight. Warm, golden, aggressively cheerful sunlight, pouring through the warped glass of the window and landing directly on my face, like it had been aiming.

I was on the floor. On my back. Tangled in the wool blanket, which I didn't remember taking with me, with my head next to the chamber pot, which I was choosing not to think about.

My mouth tasted like pennies and pond water.

That was when I remembered what I'd put in it.

"Oh no," I said to the ceiling.

I had eaten the lure.

I had eaten a glowing, ancient, possibly cursed fishing lure, in the middle of the night, because a voice in a dream told me to. Like a shot of tequila. With a *slurp.*

There was a whole list of decisions in my life I wasn't proud of. That one had just gone straight to the top.

*Bang. Bang.*

"Eli! *Sunrise!* Remember?"

"Coming!" I croaked. "I'm coming!"

I sat up.

The writing was still there.

⸻

It hung in the air about a foot in front of my face. Lines of soft blue light, the same blue as the lure, floating perfectly still no matter how I moved my head.

Last night, it had been gibberish. Strange, careful little markings. The same ones inside the lure. The same ones on the back of Grandpa's photograph.

I watched them change.

They didn't fade. They *rearranged.* The marks slid and turned and folded into each other, like they'd noticed who was reading them and were politely switching languages.

Then I could read them.

> **Region Core: Reedwater** has been consumed.
>
> The land has accepted you.
>
> You are now bound to **Reedwater**.

I stared at it.

It stared back, in the way that text does.

"Okay," I said. "Okay. Sure."

I'd played enough games to recognize what this was. I'd played enough *bad* games to recognize what this was. I had spent an embarrassing percentage of my twenties reading web novels about guys who got hit by trucks and woke up with floating blue screens.

I had always assumed, very confidently, that if it ever happened to me, I'd handle it with dignity.

I poked it.

My finger went straight through. The words rippled like the surface of a pond and then settled again, entirely unbothered.

> **Bound abilities**
>
> **[Still Water]** — *Awakened.* Your mind is a lake. Panic is a stone. Let it sink.
>
> **[Reedwater's Call]** — *Dormant.* Requires resonance.
>
> **[???]** — *Locked.*
>
> **[???]** — *Locked.*

"Still Water," I read out loud. "Great. My superpower is being calm."

"It would be a considerable improvement," said a voice from the corner.

⸻

I didn't scream.

I want that on the record.

I made a sound. It was high-pitched and it lasted longer than I would've liked, but it was not, technically, a scream.

The ocelot was still there.

It was sitting in the exact same spot as last night, in the corner by the stool, with its tail wrapped neatly around its front paws. Spotted. Sleek. Golden-brown, with dark rosettes running along its back and two black stripes down each cheek, like somebody had drawn on its eyeliner with a very steady hand.

Its eyes were blue.

Not ocelot blue. Not any kind of animal blue.

*Core* blue.

"You," I said.

"Me," it agreed.

"You talked. Last night. You talked, and then I passed out."

"You did," it said. "Face first. Into the stool. I did try to warn you, but you seemed very committed."

Its voice was low and smooth and faintly bored, like a butler who had been forced to work for a family he didn't respect. It had an accent I couldn't place. It sounded like it was enjoying this.

"What *are* you?"

"An ocelot."

"Ocelots don't talk."

It looked at me for a long, slow moment, the way cats do when you've said something so stupid it isn't worth blinking at.

"You are sitting on the floor of another world," it said, "with a Region Core in your stomach, reading words that are hanging in the air in front of you. And the part you've decided to object to is the talking cat."

I opened my mouth.

I closed it again.

That was fair.

"Okay," I said. "Fine. Do you have a name?"

"I do."

I waited.

It cleaned one paw.

"…Are you going to *tell* me it?"

"No," said the ocelot.

"Why not?"

"Because names have weight, and you couldn't carry a waterskin up a hill." It set its paw down. "Earn it."

> **[Still Water]** has activated.

Something cool slid through the middle of my chest, like I'd stepped into a lake on a hot day. My heart, which had been doing its best impression of a drum solo since I woke up, slowed down. Not all the way. But enough.

I took a breath.

It was a much better breath than the one before it.

"Huh," I said.

"Yes," said the ocelot. "That one's going to get a lot of use."

*Bang. Bang. BANG.*

"*Eli!* I swear, if you don't open this door, I'm sending in the Rimejaw!"

I looked at the door. I looked at the ocelot.

"She's going to see you," I said.

"Will she?" It sounded genuinely curious.

⸻

I got my jeans on, mostly. I opened the door.

Mara was standing there with her fist raised to knock again. She was dressed for training. Leather vest, sleeves rolled to the elbow, hair pulled back so tight it looked painful. The scratch on her cheekbone had scabbed over. She looked like she'd been up for hours and had spent every one of them getting angrier at me.

"*Finally,*" she said. "Come on, sleepyhead. Grandma's waiting, and Drayfus hates waiting even more."

Then she actually looked at me.

She frowned.

"Why are you sweating? Why were you on the *floor*? I could hear you yelling all the way from the house."

"Bad dream," I said. Which was true. It just wasn't the whole truth.

I stepped aside a little, so she could see into the room. Into the corner.

"Hey. Quick question. Totally normal question." I pointed. "Do you see the cat?"

Mara looked at the corner.

The ocelot looked at Mara.

Mara looked at me.

"What cat?"

"Ocelot," said the ocelot.

She didn't react. Not even a flicker. She was standing six feet from a talking spotted cat with glowing eyes, and she was looking at me exactly the way you'd look at a guy who'd just asked if you could see the cat.

"There's no cat, Eli."

"Right," I said. "No. Of course. There's no cat."

"Are you sure you're okay?"

"I'm great." I pulled on my shoe. "Never been better. Really."

Behind her, the ocelot stood up, stretched, and walked straight toward me, and as it stepped into the patch of sunlight on the floor, it came apart. Not violently. It just *thinned,* like a reflection in a pond when someone drags a finger through the water, and then it was a scatter of blue motes drifting in the light, and then it was nothing at all.

Something warm settled low in my stomach. Right where the lure had gone.

"Only you," said its voice, very quietly, from somewhere much closer than the corner. "Only ever you. Do try to keep up."

I decided, for my own sanity, that this was a problem for future me.

Future me was really starting to pile up.

⸻

The training ground was a circle of packed dirt behind the chief's house, fenced in by a low ring of split logs. There was a weapon rack against the back wall, a row of straw dummies in various states of having been murdered, and a water trough that I was fairly sure was going to be very important to me in about twenty minutes.

Emilia was sitting on a bench in the shade with a cup of tea, like she'd come out to watch a play.

And standing in the middle of the circle, with his arms folded, was the man from yesterday.

The one with the scars. And the sword.

Up close, he was even bigger. Broad through the chest and shoulders in a way that didn't come from a gym. Short dark hair going gray at the sides. A pale scar across his jaw and another along his forearm, and a dozen smaller ones that looked like the kind of thing he didn't bother counting anymore. The very big sword was strapped across his back. It was taller than Mara.

He looked at the sky. Then he looked at me.

"Sunrise," he said, "was some time ago."

"Sorry," I said. "I had a, uh. Rough night."

"So did the Rimejaw we buried yesterday."

Okay.

So that was how this was going to go.

"Drayfus," Emilia said mildly, from her bench.

He didn't look at her. He kept looking at me. It was the same measuring look from yesterday, the one that worked out how long I'd last in a fight, except now he'd had a whole night to think about it and the number hadn't improved.

"Drayfus," he said. "I lead the village guard. I fought my first shadow walker when I was younger than her." He tipped his head at Mara. "Your grandfather taught me how to hold a blade. So the chief tells me I owe him."

Something in my chest went tight. *Still Water* didn't do anything about it. I guess it wasn't that kind of feeling.

"You knew him?"

"I knew him." Drayfus unfolded his arms. "And you are nothing like him. Pick up a sword."

⸻

I would like to say I held my own.

I would like to say a lot of things.

He gave me a wooden practice sword, which was heavier than it looked, and he picked up one for himself, which looked like a toothpick in his hand. He told me to attack him. I did my best.

My best lasted about two seconds.

I swung. He wasn't there anymore. Something tapped me on the back of the knee, very gently, and the ground came up and hit me in the face.

> You have taken damage.

"Thanks," I told the floating words, into the dirt. "Really helpful."

"Up," said Drayfus.

I got up. I swung again. This time I didn't even see what hit me. One second I was standing, and the next I was lying on my back looking at the sky, which was a very nice blue, and my sword was somewhere over by the fence.

> You have taken damage.

"Up."

Mara was sitting on the fence, swinging her legs. She had her hand over her mouth. I could see her shoulders shaking.

"You're *laughing*," I said.

"I'm not," she said, laughing.

I went down nine times.

On the tenth, my legs just sort of decided to stop being involved, and I stayed down. I lay there in the dirt with my lungs on fire and my arms full of sand, staring up at the sky, and I thought about how the last time I'd done any real exercise was carrying groceries up three flights of stairs, and Shylee had carried half of them.

> **[Still Water]** has activated.

The cool feeling slid through my chest again, and the panic sank, like a stone.

And without the panic in the way, I started noticing things.

The way Drayfus's feet never crossed. The way he always moved *before* I swung, like he was reading something. The way, every single time, he waited for me to commit and then simply wasn't where I'd committed to.

*He's watching my shoulders,* I thought. *I telegraph everything with my shoulders.*

It didn't help. Knowing why you're losing and being able to stop losing are two very different things. But it was the first thing in this world that had made sense to me in a while, and I held on to it.

A shadow fell across my face.

"You stopped flinching," Drayfus said. He was frowning down at me. "Around the sixth time. Why?"

I hesitated.

Then I looked past him, at Emilia on her bench, who had stopped drinking her tea and was watching me very closely.

I thought about the lure. How I'd kept it hidden from Mara at the campfire, on instinct, and how fast that secret had fallen apart anyway.

"I ate it," I said.

⸻

There was a silence.

It was a long one.

Mara stopped swinging her legs.

"You *what?*"

"The Region Core. Last night." I sat up, which took a while. "Your grandma said I had to ingest it, so I… ingested it."

"You *ate* it?"

"You were both *there!* She *told* me to!"

"I didn't think you'd actually *do* it! Not on the first *night!* Not *alone!*" Mara jumped down off the fence. "You said you'd think about it! People think about it for *months!* Grandma, tell him people think about it for months!"

"People think about it for months," Emilia said.

She'd set her tea down on the bench. She got up, slowly, and came across the training circle, and crouched in front of me, which her knees had clear opinions about. She looked into my face. Into my eyes, specifically, like she was looking for something in there.

"What did you see?" she asked quietly. "After."

"Writing," I said. "Floating. Right in front of me. I couldn't read it at first, and then it…" I waved a hand. "Changed. Now I can."

"What does it say?"

I squinted at the empty air. The words were still sitting there, waiting, very patient.

"It says the land accepted me," I said. "And I've got a power called Still Water, which, as far as I can tell, means I'm really good at not panicking. And some other ones that are locked."

Drayfus snorted.

Emilia didn't.

Her eyes had gone bright and wet. She was smiling, but it was a smile that hurt.

"Oscar used to do that," she said. "Stop in the middle of a sentence and read something that wasn't there. Sometimes he'd laugh at it. Sometimes he'd argue with it." She shook her head. "We used to tease him about it. We thought it was the strangest thing in the world."

I stared at her.

Grandpa.

Grandpa, in his fishing jacket, sitting on the dock and staring off at the trees like he was waiting for someone who was late.

Maybe he wasn't staring at the trees.

Maybe he was reading.

"Did he ever see a cat?" I asked, before I could stop myself.

Emilia blinked. "A what?"

"Nothing. Never mind. I hit my head on a stool."

From somewhere low in my stomach, I felt something that I can only describe as a very smug purr.

Then the ocelot's voice, quiet, just for me.

"I would keep the girl to yourself, if I were you."

I went still.

*The girl.*

White hair. Purple eyes. Crying.

*Croshelda.*

"Not everyone in this village has forgotten what that name cost them," the ocelot said. "And you don't yet know what it means that you screamed it."

I looked at Emilia's kind, worried face. I looked at Drayfus, and the scars, and thought about the Rimejaw they'd buried yesterday.

I didn't say anything.

I was getting a lot of practice at that.

⸻

"Well," Drayfus said finally. "That changes nothing."

"Drayfus," Emilia said again.

"It changes *nothing,*" he said. "A core gives you a door. It doesn't walk you through it. He still can't hold a sword. He still can't run a mile. And if a shadow walker comes for him tonight, he'll still be dead, only he'll be dead with something very valuable in his stomach, which they will open him up to get." He looked down at me. "Do you understand what I'm saying?"

"…That I should be scared?"

"That you should get *up.*"

I got up.

It was not graceful.

Drayfus walked over to the weapon rack and stood in front of it. Swords. Spears. A couple of axes. A bow with a cracked limb. Things I didn't have names for.

"The sword is wrong for you," he said, without turning around. "Your grip is wrong. Your feet are wrong. You fight like a man trying to swat a bee. So." He stepped aside. "Choose."

I limped over to the rack.

I looked at the swords. Then the spears. Then the axes, which I immediately ruled out on the grounds that the last guy I saw holding one had tried to kill me.

And then I saw it, leaning in the corner at the very end of the rack, behind a broken spear shaft, where someone had set it down carefully and then forgotten about it.

Grandpa's rod.

The old one. The one from the cabin. The one Mara had been using to cook breakfast over a campfire the morning after the storm.

I looked at Mara.

She shrugged, a little embarrassed. "I brought it back from the shore. I didn't want it to get stolen. It seemed important to you."

It was.

I reached for it.

The second my fingers closed around the cork grip, the core in my stomach *thrummed,* like a plucked string. Warmth ran up my arm. The rod didn't light up or anything. It was still just a scratched old fishing rod with fresh line on it.

But the words in front of my face changed.

> Resonance detected.
>
> **[Reedwater's Call]** — *Awakened.*
>
> The water remembers those who are patient with it. Cast, and it will answer.

"Oh," I said softly.

Behind me, Drayfus laughed.

It was the first time I'd heard him laugh. It wasn't a mean laugh, exactly. It was the laugh of a man who had been asked to train a guy who couldn't hold a sword, and had just watched him pick a fishing rod off a weapon rack.

"That," he said, "is not a weapon."

"It's what I've got."

"It's a *stick* with *string* on it."

He was still smiling when he picked up his practice sword and walked back to the middle of the circle. He didn't even bother raising it. He just stood there, loose and easy, waiting for me to embarrass myself for the eleventh time.

Mara had climbed back onto the fence. She wasn't laughing now. She was watching the rod.

So was Emilia.

I walked to the edge of the circle.

> **[Still Water]** has activated.

The cool feeling. The stone sinking. The world got very quiet, and very simple, the way it used to on the dock when I was a kid, sitting next to Grandpa with a rod that was way too big for me, waiting for something to bite.

*Patience, kiddo. The fish don't care how bad you want it.*

I didn't look at his shoulders.

I didn't look at his feet.

I looked at the sword.

And I cast.

The line sang off the reel, a bright, thin whistle, and for one heartbeat the whole training ground seemed to hold its breath. The lure-less hook flashed blue in the morning sun, the same blue as the words in the air, the same blue as the core, and the line didn't arc the way a line should arc.

It *bent.*

It curved through the air like it had somewhere to be, around Drayfus's raised guard and under his arm, and wrapped itself three times around the blade of his practice sword, neat as a knot, before he'd finished blinking.

I pulled.

The sword left his hand.

It sailed across the circle, end over end, and landed with a thump in the dirt at my feet.

Nobody said anything.

Somewhere on the roof of the chief's house, a Flitlynx stopped cleaning its paw.

I looked down at the sword. I looked at the rod in my hands. I looked at the floating text, which was just sitting there, glowing faintly, like this was the most normal thing in the world.

> **Fishing** has increased.

"…Huh," I said.

Drayfus looked at his empty hand.

Then he looked at me, and for the first time since I'd arrived in this world, the measuring look was gone. There was something else there instead. Something that looked a lot more like what was on Emilia's face when she'd first seen me.

Like he was looking at a house he used to live in.

He walked over. He picked his sword up out of the dirt. He turned it over in his hand and looked at the little burn marks the line had left on the wood, three neat rings of them, faintly glowing blue before they faded.

"Oscar," he said quietly. Just the name. Nothing else.

Then he straightened up, and his face closed again like a door.

"Again," said Drayfus.

And somewhere warm, low in my stomach, I could have sworn I heard a cat laughing.
