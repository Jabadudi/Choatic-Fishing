# Chapter 14 — The Night of Ash

The first thing I noticed was the smell.

Not smoke. Not yet. Something colder. Like a cellar that's been shut for a hundred years, and someone has just opened the door.

Then the cold came.

It rolled in over the fields like fog, except it wasn't fog. You couldn't see it. You could only feel it. The grass went stiff and gray in a line, a long, slow line, moving toward us from the east. Behind it, the dark was thicker than it should have been. Darker than night. Darker than the bottom of the lake.

And it was moving.

"Hold," said Drayfus, from the watchtower. He didn't shout. He didn't have to. In that silence, everyone heard him.

I set my feet. Legs bent. Shoulders back and down. My shield overlapped Mara's on one side and Teo's on the other.

*Your mind is a lake,* said a voice in the back of my head, and I didn't know if it was the core or the ocelot or Grandpa. *Panic is a stone. Let it sink.*

I let it sink.

It sank.

It came straight back up again, but I'll take what I can get.

⸻

"Light them," said Drayfus.

Someone threw a torch. Then another. One by one, down the long funnel of lanes we'd spent eleven days building, the fire pits caught. Dry brush and pitch went up with a sound like a giant breathing in, and suddenly Willowback was glowing. Orange and gold. A long line of fire running out from the village toward the fields, every twenty paces, just like Drayfus had drawn in the dirt.

And in that light, for the first time, I saw them.

I'd seen them in the dream. From above, from far away, like an anthill. That was not the same thing. That was not even close to the same thing.

They filled the fields.

Shadow walkers, hundreds of them, tall and thin and faceless, the dark where their faces should be drinking up the firelight. And among them, bigger shapes. A boar the size of a car, its tusks dripping black. An elk with antlers like a dead tree, its eyes full of nothing. Things on four legs and things on six and one thing, near the back, that I am still not going to describe, because I would like to sleep again someday.

They didn't charge. They didn't scream.

They just walked. Slowly. All together. Toward the fire.

"Oh," Mara said quietly beside me. "Oh, that's a lot."

"Yeah."

"That's a *lot,* Eli."

"I know."

Her shoulder pressed harder against mine.

"One breath," I said. "Then the next breath."

She let out a long, shaky laugh.

"I hate that you listened to him," she said, and set her feet.

⸻

They hit the ditch first.

The front of the tide came on at a walk, and then all at once, like something had flipped a switch, it wasn't walking anymore. A boar the size of a car came down the field at a dead run, and I watched it hit the edge of the ditch, and I watched the ground open up under it, and I watched it go down onto the stakes.

It screamed.

That was the first sound any of them had made. It was the worst sound I'd ever heard.

And then the rest of them came over it.

That's the thing about a ditch. It works. It really does. It just doesn't work forever. The shadow walkers came over the boar, and over each other, and the ditch filled up with dark, twitching shapes, and then it wasn't a ditch anymore, it was a bridge.

They came across the fire line, and the fire hurt them. You could see it. Where the light touched them they flinched and smoked and came apart at the edges like burning paper. Some of them went down and didn't get back up.

Most of them kept coming.

"*Hold!*" said Drayfus.

They hit the line.

⸻

I'm not going to pretend I remember all of it.

I remember the weight. That's the main thing. A shadow walker hitting a shield is like being hit by a car that doesn't care about you. It doesn't hate you. It doesn't want anything from you. It just wants *through,* and you are in the way, and it leans on you with everything it has.

And I held.

Legs bent. Shoulders back and down. A month of squats and laps and buckets and ditches, every single one of them, all at once, in my thighs and my back and the soles of my feet. I held, and Mara held, and Teo held, and the shield wall bent and shuddered and screamed and *held.*

*One breath.*

Mara stabbed over the top of her shield with her spear, fast, efficient, the way she did everything. Teo was shouting something I couldn't hear.

*The next breath.*

Something came over the top. A long, black arm, reaching for my face, cold so intense it burned. I didn't think about it. I didn't think about anything. My hand went over my shoulder, and closed on Grandpa's rod, and *pulled.*

The line went out blue.

It bent. It curved through the air, over the shield, around the arm, and hit the shadow walker right in the middle of where its face should have been. There was a flash like lightning on water, and the thing came apart, all at once, into black ash and cold wind.

Reserves dropped. I felt it go, like someone had pulled a plug in my stomach.

*Seven,* I thought. *Seven more, maybe. No water here. Make them count.*

I made them count.

⸻

Here's what I didn't know, holding the line in the eastern lane.

The army had come from the east, just like Drayfus said. Most of it.

Not all of it.

The first I knew of it was the howling. Not the low, mournful sound the wolves had made every night for two weeks. A different sound. Sharp. Furious. It came from the north, from the forest side, where there were no barricades and no ditch and no fire pits, because that side of the village backed onto the trees and the trees were thick and nothing that big should have been able to get through them.

Something big had gotten through them.

I turned my head. I shouldn't have, but I did. Over the rooftops, on the north side of Willowback, I saw a shape pushing through the trees. Then another. Then the first roof went up in flames, not orange-gold like our fire pits but dark red, sullen, *wrong.*

And then I saw the wolves.

Gray shapes, a dozen of them, maybe more, pouring out of the forest and throwing themselves at the things coming through the trees. In the middle of them, bigger than the rest, a silver line along his back: Brume.

He'd come.

They'd all come. Every Moonfang in the forest.

"*Drayfus!*" someone was screaming. "The north! They're in the north!"

⸻

Then the general walked out of the dark.

I don't know how else to say it. One moment the shadow walkers in front of our line were pressing and pushing and dying in the fire, and the next moment they stopped. All of them. At once. Like a held breath.

They parted.

And a man walked down the lane between them.

He was tall. Taller than Drayfus. He wore armor so dark it didn't reflect the firelight at all, it just swallowed it. And unlike the shadow walkers around him, he had a face. A real one. Pale and narrow, almost handsome, with hollow cheeks and eyes like two chips of black glass.

That was so much worse than no face at all.

He stopped twenty paces from the shield wall and looked us over, the way a man looks over a fence he's going to have to climb.

"Willowback," he said. His voice was just one voice. Calm. Almost polite. "I am Corvane, a general of the Witch of Darkness. My lady sends her regards."

Nobody said anything.

"She has a long memory," said Corvane. "And this village has something of hers. Again." His black eyes moved along the line of shields, slowly, one face at a time, until they reached me.

They stopped.

"*There* you are," he said softly. "She was right. You do shine."

⸻

"He's not yours," said Emilia.

I hadn't seen her come. None of us had. She was just there, suddenly, walking down the lane behind our line, in her shawl and her long silver hair, with a staff of pale wood in her hand that I had never once seen her carry.

The shield wall opened for her without anyone telling it to.

She walked through it. Past me. Past Mara, who made a sound like she'd been hit. Out into the empty ground between us and the general, until she was standing alone in the firelight, ten paces from him.

"Grandma," said Mara. "Grandma, *no*—"

"He's not yours," Emilia said again. "He never was. Neither was the child a man once carried into this village, wrapped in a blanket, on a night a lot like this one." She lifted her chin. "You've had a long walk, general. Go home and tell her that."

Corvane smiled.

"Old woman," he said, almost gently. "You remember. Good. Then you know how this ends."

"Yes," said Emilia. "I do."

And she lifted her staff.

⸻

I'd known she was more than she seemed.

Everyone in that village knew. You could see it in the way they stepped aside for her, the way Drayfus straightened when she spoke, the way even the ocelot went quiet and careful when she looked toward the hearthstones. I'd known she was more than a kind old woman with a clay teapot and bad knees.

I hadn't known she was *this.*

Light came out of her.

Not fire. Not lightning. Light, pure and white and silver, like moonlight if the moon were standing right in front of you. It poured out of the staff and out of her hands and out of her *eyes,* and it hit the shadow walkers like a wave hitting sand.

They didn't burn. They *vanished.* The front ranks just stopped existing, a whole street's width of them, gone in a gust of black ash. The infected elk behind them reared and screamed and came apart. The light rolled down the lane and out across the fields, and everywhere it touched, the dark shrank away from it.

For one moment, in the middle of the worst night of my life, it was bright as noon in Willowback.

I could see her face. She wasn't afraid.

She looked young.

Corvane threw up an arm. The light broke around him like water around a rock. His dark armor smoked and hissed, and for the first time, the calm went out of his face.

"Drayfus," said Emilia. She didn't turn around. Her voice was perfectly steady. "Call the water."

From the watchtower, Drayfus said nothing for one long second.

Then he called it.

"*THE WATER! EVERYONE TO THE WATER!*"

⸻

The line broke.

Not because we lost. Because we let it go. The shield wall came apart and people ran, the way they'd practiced, eleven days of running in the dark until the kids could do it without crying. Toward the docks. Toward the boats with their oars already inside.

Down the lane, a red-faced man with a bandaged arm went by me at a dead run with four kids clinging to him. The littlest one was over his shoulder, holding on to his collar with both fists.

Hobb didn't look at me.

He didn't need to.

And Emilia stood in the middle of the lane, alone, with the light pouring out of her and the whole army of the Witch of Darkness pressing in on every side, and she held.

One breath.

Then the next.

"Eli."

Mara had my arm. Her face was wet. I don't know when she'd started crying. I don't know when I had.

"Eli, we have to go. She said— you *promised*—"

I'd promised.

*If the line breaks, you take Mara and you go. North. Not to the boats. Away. You don't look back for anything. Not for a pot, not for a goat, not for a person.*

*Not for you.*

*Not for me.*

The ocelot was at my feet. I hadn't seen it come. Its blue eyes were very wide.

"Go," it said. "Eli. *Go.*"

I went.

⸻

We ran north. Up the lane, past the lodge, toward the burning roofs and the howling and the trees, with the fire on every side and the cold behind us, and Mara's hand in mine.

I kept my promise.

Mostly.

I looked back once.

Just once. At the top of the lane, where it bent around the corner of the lodge, right before the light would have been gone from view. I didn't decide to. My head just turned.

She was still standing there.

Small. So small, in the middle of all that dark. The light around her was shrinking now, pulling in, a circle of silver getting tighter and tighter as the shadows leaned on it from every side. And Corvane was walking toward her through it, slowly, one step at a time, with his armor smoking and his face set like stone.

She didn't step back.

She looked over her shoulder, once. Not at Corvane. At the docks. At the long line of boats already pulling out onto the black water, full of people. Children. Old men. Hobb and his four kids.

She smiled.

Then she turned back, and she raised the staff over her head with both hands, and all the light she had left went up at once, in a single column, straight up into the sky, so bright that I had to look away.

When I looked back, the light was gone.

The lane was dark.

And Emilia wasn't standing anymore.

⸻

I don't remember making a sound. Mara says I did. Mara says I tried to go back.

I believe her.

I remember her hands, both of them, fisted in my shirt. I remember her pulling. I remember her saying my name, over and over, and something else, which I didn't understand until much later, which was *she did it, she did it, Eli, look at the water, she did it.*

Out on the lake, the boats were still moving. Every one of them.

None of the shadow walkers had followed them. They were standing at the water's edge. Hundreds of them. Just standing there, the way they'd stood in the valley in my dream, looking out at the boats they couldn't reach.

She'd bought them the water.

She'd bought them all the water.

Behind us, Willowback was burning. Dark red fire, the wrong kind, crawling from roof to roof. Ash was falling, gray and soft, like snow. It caught in Mara's hair. It caught in mine.

And somewhere back down the lane, in the dark, a calm voice said, almost politely:

"Find the boy."
